# Welcome to My Research Blog

Welcome to my research blog.

I am **Yoshiaki Chiku, MD**, an ophthalmologist and clinical researcher at Shinshu University School of Medicine, Japan. My research focuses on retinal imaging, particularly wide-field optical coherence tomography (OCT), quantitative image analysis, and reproducible clinical research.

I created this website as a place to openly share my research activities, ideas, and technical resources. While scientific papers remain the primary way to communicate research findings, they often cannot include all of the code, implementation details, and practical knowledge developed during a project. My goal is to make those resources more accessible to researchers, clinicians, and students who are interested in ophthalmic imaging and quantitative analysis.

## What You Will Find Here

This website will gradually grow into a collection of resources, including:

* Research updates and project summaries
* Publications and supplementary materials
* Open-source R code and analysis workflows
* Tutorials on statistical methods and reproducible research
* Educational notes on retinal imaging and OCT
* Reflections on clinical research and scientific writing

## Why Open Science?

I strongly believe that research should be transparent and reproducible whenever possible.

For many of my projects, I plan to share the statistical analysis code used in the published studies through GitHub. Although patient data cannot be made publicly available because of ethical and institutional restrictions, I hope that sharing code and example datasets will help others understand, reproduce, and build upon the analytical methods.

Open science benefits not only individual researchers but also the scientific community as a whole by improving transparency, reproducibility, and collaboration.

## Looking Ahead

This blog is a long-term project.

Over the coming years, I hope to document my research journey, introduce useful analytical techniques, and provide practical resources for ophthalmic researchers. I also hope that this website will become a platform for future collaborations with researchers around the world.

Thank you for visiting.

I hope you find something useful here, and I look forward to sharing my work with you.
